Planet Earth

REMANENCEEvery journey into the cold dark began here.

From this fragile blue marble against the dark, humanity dared to cast metal, glass, and collective memory into the vacuum. We sent our mechanical emissaries across the void to see what human eyes could not yet reach.

01 THE MOON · MARE TRANQUILLITATIS

Apollo 11 Descent Stage

MACHINELM-5 “Eagle” Descent Stage
LANDEDJuly 20, 1969 · Tranquility Base (0.674° N, 23.473° E)
STATUSSILENT
REMANENCEGold Kapton foil, stainless steel plaque, bootprints in vacuum.

Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin brought Eagle down with seconds of fuel remaining. When the ascent engine fired to bring them home, the descent stage remained. Bolted to its forward strut is a plaque: “Here men from the planet Earth first set foot upon the Moon. We came in peace for all mankind.” In the airless stillness, its gold foil glints in unfiltered sunlight.

02 THE MOON · HADLEY RILLE & TAURUS-LITTROW

Lunar Roving Vehicles

MACHINELRV-1, LRV-2, LRV-3 (Apollo 15, 16, 17)
LAST CONTACTDecember 14, 1972 (Apollo 17 Departure)
STATUSSILENT
REMANENCEThree electric rovers parked toward the stars; tire tracks crisp in the dust.

Between 1971 and 1972, three electric buggies carried humanity across ancient lunar valleys. Commander Eugene Cernan parked LRV-3 a short distance away to capture the final liftoff on camera, wiping dust from the lens one last time. No human hand has touched its steering joystick in over fifty years.

03 THE MOON · OCEANUS PROCELLARUM

Surveyor 3

MACHINESurveyor 3 Robotic Soft Lander
VISITEDNovember 19, 1969 by Apollo 12 (Conrad & Bean)
STATUSSILENT
REMANENCEAluminum frame resting on the inner slope of Surveyor Crater.

Two years after Surveyor 3 bounced to rest inside a gentle crater, Apollo 12 astronauts Pete Conrad and Alan Bean pinpoint-landed within walking distance. They walked down the slope to inspect the machine, clipping its television camera to bring home to Earth. It remains the only time humans have revisited a robot on another world.

INTERPLANETARY VOID // 225 MILLION KILOMETERS

The Crossing

Between the silver dust of the Moon and the rusted sands of Mars lies months of profound silence. Only the quiet ping of deep-space telemetry carries through the void, linking fragile machines across the dark to their architects on Earth.

04 MARS · CHRYSE PLANITIA

Viking 1 Lander

MACHINEViking 1 Mars Lander
TOUCHDOWNJuly 20, 1976 · Plain of Gold (22.697° N, 48.222° W)
LAST CONTACTNovember 11, 1982 (2,245 Sols of Operation)
REMANENCEThree-legged lander resting beneath red iron dunes; soil trench open.

On the seventh anniversary of Apollo 11, Viking 1 touched the red soil of Chryse Planitia and transmitted humanity’s first clear photograph from the surface of Mars. For over six Earth years, it faithfully measured the Martian weather until a ground software upload accidentally overwrote its antenna coordinates. It still listens for a signal that will never come.

05 MARS · ARES VALLIS

Pathfinder & Sojourner

MACHINECarl Sagan Memorial Station & Sojourner Microrover
TOUCHDOWNJuly 4, 1997 · Ancient Floodplain (19.33° N, 33.55° W)
LAST CONTACTSeptember 27, 1997
REMANENCEAirbag petals and a microwave-sized rover parked by rock “Yogi”.

Bouncing into an ancient flood plain protected by giant airbags, Pathfinder unfurled three solar petals to release Sojourner—the first wheeled rover on an extraterrestrial planet. When the base station battery finally expired in autumn 1997, Sojourner executed its programmed failsafe: circling the silent lander repeatedly until its own solar panel went cold.

06 MARS · GUSEV CRATER

Spirit Rover (MER-A)

MACHINEMars Exploration Rover “Spirit”
TRAPPEDMay 2009 in sulfate sand pit “Troy”
LAST CONTACTMarch 22, 2010 (Sol 2210)
REMANENCESix aluminum wheels embedded in hidden pale sulfates at Home Plate.

Designed for 90 days, Spirit explored Gusev Crater for six years. Even when its right front wheel seized, engineers drove the rover backward, dragging the dead wheel across the soil—which inadvertently carved open brilliant white deposits of pure silica, proving volcanic hot springs once bubbled on Mars. Entrenched in loose sand at Troy, it greeted its final freezing winter.

07 MARS · ENDEAVOUR CRATER

Opportunity Rover (MER-B)

MACHINEMars Exploration Rover “Opportunity”
DISTANCE45.16 Kilometers (Off-Earth Driving Record)
LAST CONTACTJune 10, 2018 (Sol 5111)
FINAL TELEMETRY“My battery is low and it is getting dark.”
REMANENCESolar arrays blanketed under perpetual Endeavour silt.

For nearly fifteen years, Opportunity traversed the Meridiani plains. In June 2018, a catastrophic global dust storm choked the Martian sky, blotting out 99% of sunlight. Its final transmission reported an atmospheric opacity tau exceeding 10.8 and power falling to 22 watt-hours. Over eight months, NASA transmitted more than a thousand recovery commands into the silence. None were answered.

08 MARS · JEZERO CRATER

Ingenuity Mars Helicopter

MACHINEMars Rotorcraft “Ginny”
RECORD72 Flights Completed · 17.0 km Covered
FINAL FLIGHTJanuary 18, 2024 · Retired Honorably
REMANENCECarbon-fiber blades resting upright on Valinor Hills sand.

Carrying a postage-stamp swatch of fabric from the Wright Brothers’ 1903 Flyer, Ingenuity proved powered aerodynamic flight is possible in Mars’ razor-thin atmosphere. Planned for five test hops, the 1.8-kg drone flew 72 missions. On its final touchdown in January 2024, rotor blade tip damage concluded its aerial journey. Today, it serves as a permanent automated weather outpost greeting the Martian dawn.

09 THE ENDURING SIGNAL · APOLLO 11, 14, 15

The Answering Signal

Fifty years after human boots left the regolith, there is one Apollo experiment that has never gone silent. The Lunar Laser Ranging Retroreflectors require no batteries, no solar cells, and no computers. Observatories in Texas, France, and Hawaii still fire high-powered green lasers through telescopes directly at these silent quartz prisms, catching single returning photons to measure the Moon’s distance to millimeter precision.
