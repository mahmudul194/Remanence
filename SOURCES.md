## 🌕 1. Apollo 11 Lunar Module Descent Stage (*Eagle*)

### Coordinates & Site
- **Landing Site:** Mare Tranquillitatis (Sea of Tranquility), Moon
- **Lunar Coordinates:** 0.67408° N, 23.47297° E
- **Touchdown Time:** July 20, 1969 — 20:17:40 UTC
- **Ascent Stage Liftoff:** July 21, 1969 — 17:54:00 UTC
- **Descent Stage Status:** Silent artifact on the lunar surface (57 years in vacuum)

### Engineering Blueprint Specifications
- **Manufacturer:** Grumman Aircraft Engineering Corporation (Bethpage, NY)
- **Descent Stage Geometry:** Octagonal prismatic center body, 4.22 m (166 in) width across flats, 1.65 m (65 in) height.
- **Landing Gear Stance:** 9.45 m (31 ft) diagonal span across footpads.
- **Footpads:** 0.94 m (37 in) diameter spun aluminum reverse-dish pads with crushable aluminum honeycomb shock-absorption cores.
- **Lunar Contact Sensing Probes:** 1.70 m (5.6 ft) spring-steel wire feelers extending downward from the footpad rims.
  - *Historical Note:* The forward (+Z) probe was intentionally removed to eliminate a tripping hazard for astronauts descending the ladder. The other three probes touched the lunar surface first, triggering the blue cockpit light ("Contact light," Aldrin called out, followed by "Shutdown").
- **Descent Propulsion System (DPS):** TRW Systems throttleable hypergolic rocket engine (9,870 lbf max thrust). Gimbaled titanium casing with Columbium (niobium) radiation-cooled nozzle extension skirt with characteristic titanium heat scorch discoloration. Ground clearance at touchdown: ~30 cm.
- **Astronaut Ladder:** Mounted over the forward (+Z) primary strut. 9 machined aluminum rungs, top egress porch platform.
- **Commemorative Plaque:** Stainless steel plate (7.62 × 9.0 in) mounted on the forward landing gear strut between the 3rd and 4th rungs:
  > *"HERE MEN FROM THE PLANET EARTH FIRST SET FOOT UPON THE MOON // JULY 1969, A. D. // WE CAME IN PEACE FOR ALL MANKIND"*  
  > Signed by Neil A. Armstrong, Michael Collins, Edwin E. Aldrin, Jr., and Richard Nixon.
- **MESA (Modular Equipment Stowage Assembly):** Hinged equipment pallet on Quad 4 quadrant. Deployed by Neil Armstrong by lanyard at ladder egress, exposing the Westinghouse monochrome lunar TV camera (which broadcast first steps to 650 million people).
- **Quad 1 Scientific Bay:** Contained the Early Apollo Scientific Experiments Package (EASEP) and the graphite thermal containment cask for the SNAP-27 radioisotope thermoelectric generator.

### Primary Historical Documentation & Citations
1. **NASA Manned Spacecraft Center:** *Apollo 11 Mission Report* (MSC-01855), Houston, Texas, November 1969.
2. **Grumman Aerospace Corporation:** *Apollo Operations Handbook: Lunar Module LM 10 and Subsequent* (LMA790-3-LM), 1970.
3. **NASA History Division:** *Apollo Lunar Surface Journal (ALSJ)*, edited by Eric M. Jones. Transcript of EVA radio communications and photographic records.
4. **Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera (LROC):** Narrow Angle Camera (NAC) high-resolution orbital imagery M175139055RE of Tranquility Base showing the descent stage, shadow, flag, and astronaut traverse trails.
5. **NASA 3D Resources:** Lunar Module descent stage reference models and high-resolution textures (NASA Public Domain).

## 🔬 Material Science & Weathering Basis
- **Multilayer Insulation (MLI):** Hand-wrapped embossed Kapton (polyimide) foil coated with vacuum-deposited aluminum on one side (warm gold / amber reflections).
- **Inconel & Pyromark Shielding:** High-temperature nickel-chromium alloy sheets with high-emissivity black Pyromark paint for thermal radiating surfaces.
- **Structural Framing:** 7075-T6 anodized aluminum alloy tubular trusses.
- **Regolith Adhesion (Remanence Effect):** Lunar regolith consists of jagged, unweathered glass shards and basaltic silicate dust created by billions of years of micrometeorite bombardment. Without an atmosphere or liquid water, electrostatic charging causes fine regolith dust (sub-20 micron) to cling aggressively to all upward-facing surfaces and crevices.
- **Solar UV & Micrometeorite Weathering:** Decades of unshielded vacuum exposure subject the hardware to thermal cycles (-130°C to +120°C every 29.5 Earth days), solar UV bleaching of pigments, and micro-cratering from interplanetary dust particles.
