# REMANENCE: Sources, Technical Archives & Historical Citations

Every mission log, coordinate, dimension, subsystem name, and material property in **REMANENCE** is verified against official NASA historical records, engineering blueprints, and scientific archives.

## 🌕 THE LUNAR RECORD

### 1. Apollo 11 Lunar Module Descent Stage (*Eagle*)
- **Landing Site:** Mare Tranquillitatis (Sea of Tranquility), Moon
- **Lunar Coordinates:** 0.67408° N, 23.47297° E
- **Touchdown Date:** July 20, 1969 — 20:17:40 UTC
- **Ascent Liftoff:** July 21, 1969 — 17:54:00 UTC
- **Current Status:** Resting in permanent vacuum at Tranquility Base.
- **Hardware Architecture:** Octagonal Grumman descent stage (4.22m width, 1.65m height) wrapped in embossed gold/amber Kapton MLI and black Inconel foil. Spun aluminum reverse-dish footpads (0.94m diameter) with crushable aluminum honeycomb cartridges and 3 contact sensing feeler probes. TRW Descent Propulsion System (DPS) gimbaled columbium nozzle skirt. 9-rung astronaut ladder with stainless steel commemorative plaque: *"HERE MEN FROM THE PLANET EARTH FIRST SET FOOT UPON THE MOON // JULY 1969, A. D. // WE CAME IN PEACE FOR ALL MANKIND"*. Westinghouse monochrome TV camera inside the MESA pallet.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - NASA Manned Spacecraft Center: *Apollo 11 Mission Report* (MSC-01855), Houston, TX (1969).
  - Grumman Aerospace Corporation: *Apollo Operations Handbook: Lunar Module LM 10 and Subsequent* (LMA790-3-LM).
  - NASA Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera (LROC): High-resolution NAC frame M175139055RE.

### 2. Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package (ALSEP) & SNAP-27 RTG
- **Sites:** Apollo 12, 14, 15, 16, and 17 deployment sites across the Moon
- **Central Station Coordinates (Apollo 12):** 3.0124° S, 23.4216° W
- **Operational Duration:** 1969–1977 (Commanded into final silence Sept 30, 1977)
- **Hardware Architecture:** Bendix Central Station electronics enclosure wrapped in embossed gold Kapton with white specular radiator roof plate. Deployable 0.95m S-band helical telemetry antenna mast with copper spiral element. SNAP-27 Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator (RTG) fueled by 3.8 kg Plutonium-238 inside a finned beryllium-copper cylinder with 8 radial heat dissipation fins, connected to the Central Station by a flat 3-meter fluoropolymer ribbon cable resting across the regolith. Passive Seismic Experiment (PSE) under an aluminized Mylar thermal shroud.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - Bendix Aerospace Systems Division: *ALSEP Flight System Familiarization Manual* (AL-2300).
  - NASA Johnson Space Center: *ALSEP Termination Report* (NASA Reference Publication 1036, 1979).

### 3. Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV)
- **Sites:** Apollo 15 (Hadley-Apennine), Apollo 16 (Descartes), Apollo 17 (Taurus-Littrow)
- **Apollo 17 LRV-003 Coordinates:** 20.1908° N, 30.7717° E
- **Total Distance Traversed (LRV-003):** 35.89 km
- **Hardware Architecture:** Boeing & Delco Electronics articulated 2219 tubular aluminum chassis (3.10m length, 2.06m width). 4 independently driven woven zinc-coated wire-mesh wheels (0.81m diameter) with inner titanium chevron cleats, spun aluminum disc hubs, and 8 mounting lug bolts. Dual folding tubular lawn-chair crew seats with olive woven nylon restraint webbing. T-handle 4-way drive stick and center D&C console with mechanical attitude ball and shadow-cast sun compass. Steerable 0.85m High-Gain parabolic mesh dish antenna and RCA Color Television Camera (CTC-1). Rear geological pallet with sample rake, tongs, and core tubes. Right-rear fender taped chronomap repair performed by Gene Cernan and Harrison Schmitt on Apollo 17.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - Boeing Aerospace: *Lunar Roving Vehicle Operations Handbook* (LS006-002-2H, 1971).
  - NASA Marshall Space Flight Center: *Lunar Roving Vehicle Final Technical Report* (MSFC-72-132).
  - NASA Apollo Lunar Surface Journal: Apollo 17 Traverse Maps & Audio Logs.

### 4. Apollo 15 Galileo Experiment (Hammer & Feather Memorial)
- **Site:** Hadley Rille (26.1322° N, 3.6339° E)
- **Experiment Date:** August 2, 1971 — Apollo 15 EVA-3
- **Hardware Architecture:** Apollo Lunar Geologic Hammer: 0.39m knurled 2024-T4 aluminum handle with lanyard ring, solid forged stainless steel striking head with flat impact face and pick, resting semi-embedded in regolith furrow. Falcon Feather: 0.30m female white gyrfalcon feather (mascot of USAF Academy), translucent calamus quill (rachis) with delicate barbed vanes. Dropped simultaneously by Commander David Scott to physically confirm Galileo Galilei's law that bodies in a vacuum accelerate at equal rates regardless of mass. Commander Scott's Apollo A7LB spacesuit bootprints preserved in adjacent regolith.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - NASA MSC: *Apollo 15 Preliminary Science Report* (NASA SP-289, 1972).
  - Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum: Apollo Lunar Tools Collection (Catalog A19720970000).

### 5. Surveyor 3 Lunar Lander
- **Site:** Oceanus Procellarum (Ocean of Storms), Surveyor Crater
- **Lunar Coordinates:** 3.0156° S, 23.4180° W
- **Touchdown Date:** April 20, 1967
- **Apollo 12 Visit:** November 19, 1969 (Conrad & Bean landed Intrepid 180m away)
- **Hardware Architecture:** Hughes Aircraft tubular 7075 aluminum triangular spaceframe tripod (3.0m height, 4.3m landing leg stance). 3 outrigger landing legs with crushable aluminum honeycomb footpads. Steerable planar high-gain solar panel mast. Motorized pantograph surface sampler arm with collection scoop resting in a dug lunar trench. TV camera housing with azimuth mirror head (removed by Apollo 12 crew and returned to Earth for microbiological and solar wind analysis). Dual astronaut bootprint trails between Surveyor 3 and Apollo 12 LM.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - NASA JPL: *Surveyor III Mission Report: Part II. Scientific Results* (Technical Report 32-1265, 1967).
  - NASA SP-235: *Analysis of Surveyor 3 Material and Photographs Returned by Apollo 12* (1972).

### 6. Lunar Laser Ranging Retroreflector (LLRR)
- **Sites:** Apollo 11 (Tranquility Base), Apollo 14 (Fra Mauro), Apollo 15 (Hadley Rille)
- **Apollo 11 Coordinates:** 0.6734° N, 23.4730° E
- **Current Operational Status:** Fully active (only Apollo surface hardware continuously operating today)
- **Active Laser Ranging Observatories:** Apache Point Observatory (New Mexico, USA), Observatoire de la Côte d'Azur (Grasse, France), Matera Laser Ranging Observatory (Matera, Italy).
- **Hardware Architecture:** Bendix 0.74m x 0.74m white-anodized thermal aluminum pallet tilted at 45° latitude toward Earth. 10x10 array of 100 recessed high-purity Suprasil corner-cube quartz prisms (3.8 cm diameter) with individual cylindrical thermal sunshade baffles. Sun compass gnomon pin, bubble leveling indicator, astronaut deployment handle, and folding aluminum tripod legs with contact berms. Emits collimated 532nm green pulsed laser reflections back to terrestrial telescopes, measuring lunar orbital recession at 3.8 cm/year.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - NASA Goddard Space Flight Center: *Lunar Laser Ranging Experiment Final Report* (1975).
  - Apache Point Observatory Lunar Laser-ranging Operation (APOLLO) Science Archives.

## 🔴 THE MARTIAN RECORD

### 7. Mars Entry, Descent, and Landing (EDL) Debris Field
- **Sites:** Jezero Crater (Perseverance), Gale Crater (Curiosity), Meridiani Planum (Opportunity)
- **Hardware Architecture:** Jettisoned blunt 70-degree conical aeroshell/backshell (4.5m diameter) clad in SLA-561V cork-silicone ablative thermal tiles with charred aerodynamic re-entry scorch streaks from Mach 16 atmospheric entry. Crumpled rim and sheared titanium RCS thruster brackets from high-speed surface impact. Exposed umbilical avionics wiring harnesses and thermal blanket batting. 21.5-meter supersonic nylon Disk-Gap-Band parachute with authentic alternating international orange and white gores, billowing folds draped across Martian sand dunes, and braided Technora suspension lines. Terminal descent retrorocket blast burn scar in the sand.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - NASA JPL: *Mars 2020 Perseverance Entry, Descent, and Landing Telemetry Report* (2021).
  - Ingenuity Mars Helicopter Aerial Survey: Flight 26 Aerial Imagery of Jezero EDL Debris (April 2022).
  - NASA HiRISE / Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO): High-resolution orbital debris monitoring images.

### 8. Viking 1 Mars Lander
- **Site:** Chryse Planitia (Plains of Gold), Mars
- **Mars Coordinates:** 22.48° N, 312.05° E
- **Touchdown Date:** July 20, 1976 (exactly 7 years after Apollo 11)
- **Final Transmission:** November 11, 1982 (2,307 Earth days of operations)
- **Hardware Architecture:** Martin Marietta hexagonal titanium/aluminum equipment bus (1.55m diameter, 0.45m height) with top fiberglass thermal deck and gold Kapton bottom blast shield. 3 outrigger landing legs with telescoping inverted-tripod shock struts and crushable honeycomb footpads. 2 SNAP-19 Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators (RTGs) with aerodynamic finned titanium cowlings. 3-meter surface sampler boom with soil collector scoop resting in an excavated Martian sand trench. Dual facsimile scanning camera turrets, 0.76m steerable S-band high-gain parabolic dish, and meteorology boom with wind/temperature sensors. Displaced sand berms under all footpads.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - NASA Langley Research Center: *Viking 1 Lander Final Science Report* (NASA SP-425, 1977).
  - Martin Marietta Corporation: *Viking Flight Operations Technical Manual* (1976).

### 9. Mars Pathfinder Base (*Carl Sagan Memorial Station*) & Sojourner Micro-Rover
- **Site:** Ares Vallis, Chryse Planitia (Mars)
- **Mars Coordinates:** 19.33° N, 33.55° W
- **Touchdown Date:** July 4, 1997
- **Final Transmission:** September 27, 1997
- **Hardware Architecture:** Tetrahedral lander base with 3 unfolded equilateral solar array petals clad in GaAs photovoltaic cells. Deflated multi-lobed Vectran impact airbags bunched beneath petals. Stereo Imager for Mars Pathfinder (IMP) camera mast (1.0m height) with dual optics and color filter wheel. Twin metallic egress deployment ramps. Sojourner Micro-Rover (11.5 kg): 6-wheel rocker-bogie suspension with spiral-cleated aluminum wheels, flat GaAs solar array deck, and rear Alpha Proton X-ray Spectrometer (APXS) deployed directly against a nearby basalt rock (*"Yogi"*). Egress ramp tracks pressed into the Martian dust.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - NASA JPL: *Mars Pathfinder Arrival and Surface Mission Operations Report* (1998).
  - Golombek, M. P., et al.: *Overview of the Mars Pathfinder Mission and Assessment of Landing Site Predictions*, Science 278 (1997).

### 10. Mars Exploration Rovers: Spirit (*MER-A*) & Opportunity (*MER-B*)
- **Spirit Site:** Gusev Crater (14.5684° S, 175.47263° E) — Touchdown: Jan 4, 2004 — Final Contact: March 22, 2010
- **Opportunity Site:** Meridiani Planum, Endeavour Crater (1.9462° S, 354.4734° E) — Touchdown: Jan 25, 2004 — Final Contact: June 10, 2018
- **Total Traverse (Opportunity):** 45.16 km (record for any off-world wheeled vehicle)
- **Opportunity Final Telemetry:** tau 10.8 · 22 Wh (recorded during the planet-encircling 2018 dust storm; often paraphrased in popular culture as *"My battery is low and it is getting dark"*).
- **Hardware Architecture:** Triangular Warm Electronics Box (WEB) with aerogel insulation and gold Kapton thermal wrap. Functional Rocker-Bogie mobility suspension with central differential pivot and 6 spiral-cleated aluminum wheels with flexure spokes. Bat-wing solar array deck with cell gridlines and heavy Martian hematite dust layer. 1.5m Pancam/Navcam composite mast with MarsDial color calibration sundial. 5-DOF articulated robotic arm (IDD) with Mössbauer spectrometer and Rock Abrasion Tool (RAT). Spirit displays its historical right-front stuck steering actuator digging a deep sand trench (*"Troy"*), while Opportunity displays deep twin wheel tracks in the red dust.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - NASA JPL: *Mars Exploration Rover Mission Plan and Flight Telemetry Archives* (2004–2018).
  - Squyres, S. W., et al.: *The Athena Science Payload on the Mars Exploration Rovers*, JGR Planets 108 (2003).

### 11. Ingenuity Mars Helicopter (*Ginny*)
- **Site:** Jezero Crater, Valinor Hills (Mars)
- **Mars Coordinates:** 18.4447° N, 77.4508° E
- **Touchdown on Surface:** April 3, 2021 (deployed from belly of Perseverance)
- **Final Flight (Flight 72):** January 18, 2024 (Sol 1040)
- **Total Flights:** 72 successful flights (128.8 minutes flight time, 17.0 km flown, max altitude 24.0m)
- **Final Status:** Mission concluded after rotor tip damage during emergency landing on featureless sandy terrain at Valinor Hills. Remained on surface as a stationary testbed.
- **Hardware Architecture:** Coaxial counter-rotating dual rotor system (1.2m diameter) driven by dual brushless DC motors. Lightweight carbon-fiber composite blades with aerodynamically twisted chord and swashplate linkages. **Flight 72 Historical Damage:** Snapped carbon composite rotor tip with frayed fibers, and the broken blade fragment lying on the sand 15 meters away. Cubic avionics fuselage (14cm x 14cm x 14cm) wrapped in gold Kapton with corner brackets. Top monocrystalline solar array with 900 MHz monopole antenna. Downward-facing VGA navigation camera and Garmin LIDAR-Lite v3 altimeter optics. 4 carbon-composite landing legs with aluminum footpads resting in sand berms.
- **Data & Citations:**
  - NASA JPL: *Mars 2020 Ingenuity Helicopter Flight System and Flight Logs* (2021–2024).
  - NASA / JPL-Caltech / MSSS: Sol 1040 Mastcam-Z and Navcam high-resolution damage imaging of Flight 72 blade tip fracture (Jan 2024).

## 🔬 MATERIAL SCIENCE AND GLSL WEATHERING ENGINE
All hardware surfaces in REMANENCE are rendered using Three.js `MeshPhysicalMaterial` and custom GLSL vertex/fragment shader hooks (`onBeforeCompile`):
- **Electrostatic Lunar Regolith Adhesion:** Fine sub-20 micron lunar dust clings to upward-facing normal vectors (`worldNormal.y > 0.0`) and recessed cavities via procedural Perlin noise perturbation.
- **Martian Hematite Iron Oxide Encrustation:** Directional red-orange ferric dust deposition modeling wind-driven atmospheric dust storms on Mars.
- **Crinkled MLI Normal Perturbation:** Multilayer Kapton and Mylar insulation uses high-frequency procedural normal maps to simulate manual hand-wrapping and vacuum billowing.
- **Atmospheric & Solar UV Bleaching:** Specular degradation, micro-meteorite pitting, and thermal cycle fatigue.
